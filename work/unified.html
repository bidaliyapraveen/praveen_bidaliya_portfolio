Unified Advisor Experience

From a Design Vision to an AI-Ready Operational Strategy

Lead UX Designer

Duration

3 months

Platform

Web

Responsibilities

UX Strategy, Research, IA, Interaction Design, Visual Design, Design System

Tools

Overview

At Airtel, different advisor teams were using different tools to manage customer conversations and operational tasks.

This meant advisors often had to switch between systems, find customer information in different places, and repeat the same actions across platforms.

I was asked to explore what a more unified experience could look like and how AI could become part of that future.

Goal: Bring the most important advisor workflows together into one experience without trying to rebuild everything at once.

The Problem

The same customer journey often required advisors to work across multiple systems.

This created a few common problems:

  • Too much switching between tools
  • Customer information spread across different places
  • Repetitive tasks
  • Different experiences for similar work
  • Limited visibility across the customer journey

At the same time, AI was becoming an important part of the future roadmap.

But adding AI to disconnected systems would only add another layer of complexity.

The Initial Vision

The first idea was ambitious:

One unified operational experience for different advisor teams.

The vision was to bring together:

  • Customer context
  • Conversations
  • Case history
  • Tasks
  • Knowledge
  • Escalations
  • AI assistance

Instead of designing another standalone tool, I wanted to explore a common experience that could work across different teams and grow over time.

Research & Discovery

Before designing the solution, I wanted to understand how advisors actually worked.

Advisor interviews

Spoke with advisors working across different channels to understand their daily tasks, pain points, and the tools they depended on.

Stakeholder discussions

Worked with business, operations, and leadership teams to understand their goals and expectations from a unified platform.

Workflow mapping

Mapped the advisor's day-to-day journey to see where they switched systems, repeated tasks, or needed information from different places.

Existing platform review

Reviewed the current tools to understand what already existed, what was duplicated, and where the biggest gaps were.

Competitive review

Looked at enterprise support products to understand how they handled multi-channel work, customer context, tasks, and AI.

Designing the Common Layer

I mapped the advisor's complete work cycle and looked for the parts that could be shared across different workflows.

This led to a set of common building blocks:

Customer context → Conversation → History → Tasks → Actions → Knowledge → Escalation

These became the foundation for a more flexible experience.

The idea was not to make every advisor screen identical.

It was to create a common foundation that could adapt to different teams and channels.

From Patterns to Reusable Widgets

Once the common needs were clear, I translated them into reusable modules.

For example:

  • Customer information
  • Interaction history
  • Task management
  • Communication
  • Escalation
  • AI recommendations

These modules could be reused across different advisor workflows instead of designing every experience from scratch.

Why this mattered

It gave us a way to keep the experience consistent while still allowing each team to have the functionality they needed.

The Turning Point

The future-state vision was presented to leadership. The response changed the scope of the conversation.

What started as a design exploration became a larger discussion around how Airtel could create a more unified operational experience.

But there was one challenge: The full vision was too large to implement at once.

So instead of trying to solve everything together, I helped narrow the first phase.

Strategic Pivot

We looked at the different advisor channels and identified where the unified model could realistically start.

The first phase focused on:
Email + Chat + Social

These channels already had relatively structured data and more centralized workflows, making them a better starting point for introducing AI.

The approach became:
Big vision → Smaller starting point → Validate → Learn → Expand

This allowed us to test the unified experience without trying to transform the entire ecosystem at once.

The Final Solution

The final concept focused on a unified workspace for text-based advisors.

The platform introduced:

Unified Interaction Hub

One workspace to manage customer conversations across email, chat, and social channels.

Shared Customer Context

Customer information, previous interactions, cases, and timelines available without constantly switching between systems.

AI Assistance Layer

AI was built into the workflow to help advisors with:

  • Conversation summaries
  • Suggested responses and recommendations
  • Knowledge discovery
  • Faster issue resolution

The important part was that AI supported the advisor instead of becoming another tool they had to manage.

How the Experience Changed

Before: Multiple tools

→ Search for customer information
→ Switch between systems
→ Review history
→ Find knowledge
→ Take action
→ Move to another system

After: One workspace

Customer context + conversation + history + tasks + knowledge + AI assistance

The goal was to reduce the amount of work advisors had to do just to find the information they needed.

Impact

The project helped move the conversation from individual tools to a broader experience strategy.

Design impact: Created a common set of patterns that could be reused across advisor experiences.

Product impact: Defined a practical starting point for bringing multiple advisor channels into one experience.

Strategic impact: Helped shape a phased roadmap for a larger unified and AI-ready operational platform.

Most importantly, the project showed how design could help the organization think beyond individual screens and tools.

What I Learned

This project changed how I think about unification.

Unification is not about putting different screens into one platform.

It is about understanding what people are actually trying to do, finding the common patterns, and building a flexible foundation around them.

The biggest value of the project was not just the final interface. It was using design to help the organization move from:

Multiple tools → Common behaviours → One experience → A scalable future

The final experience was only the first step.

The bigger opportunity was creating a common foundation that could support more teams, more workflows, and more AI-assisted experiences over time.
